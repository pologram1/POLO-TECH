FRAS

Facial Recognition Attendance System

Creators: Miguel A. de Jesús Rosa & Ricardo J. Lopez Ortiz

Developed together for CPEN 491 — Senior Design Project I at Universidad Ana G. Méndez, Gurabo, under the mentorship of Prof. Alcides Alvear. Ricardo and Miguel share the analysis, design, and documentation work.

The proposed system captures classroom images, identifies enrolled students, records attendance, and lets the professor review and correct the results through a web interface. The motion-controlled camera is one capture option within FRAS.

  • Requirements Engineering
  • Computer Vision
  • Embedded Systems
  • Interface Design
Read the midterm design report ↗Download report (PDF)

In development. The report documents the current design; hardware, software, and university-system access have not been finalized.

In development

The midterm design report brings together the literature review, requirements, system architecture, use cases, interface wireframes, and a study of technology alternatives.

Design work documented

  • 128 requirements: 57 functional and 71 non-functional requirements.
  • Seven subsystems: login, session management, image capture, face detection and recognition, attendance recording, review and correction, and reports.
  • 12 use cases and 10 interface wireframes: workflows for professors and administrators, traced to the functional requirements.
  • Professor workflow: start and close sessions, review uncertain matches, correct records, and consult or export reports.
  • Administrator workflow: enroll facial data, synchronize university course and roster data where access is authorized, manage user accounts, and maintain backups.

Proposed targets — not measured results

The team proposes at least 90% recognition accuracy and less than 3 seconds from capture to a stored attendance record. These targets still need validation and testing.

Hardware and software under evaluation

  • Image capture: a Raspberry Pi camera module, USB webcam, network camera, or camera module on a one-axis SG90-style servo mount.
  • Processing: Raspberry Pi 3 Model B+ as a low-cost candidate, newer Raspberry Pi hardware, an AI board, a campus computer/server, or a cloud service.
  • Recognition: DeepFace is proposed as the main base to evaluate alongside OpenCV with YuNet/SFace, dlib-based face_recognition, and cloud alternatives.
  • Storage: SQLite, PostgreSQL/pgvector, and MySQL remain candidates.
  • Camera movement and sensors: horizontal servo tracking remains part of the camera concept; an optional external sensor is still under consideration, with its model and compatibility unconfirmed.

Cost, accuracy, response time, privacy, classroom coverage, maintainability, and university compatibility guide the comparison. No technology choice is presented as final.

Next development steps

  1. Review the wireframes and session workflow with professors.
  2. Refine requirements, use cases, and interfaces using that feedback.
  3. Compare compatible hardware and software combinations and select the implementation.
  4. Develop the detailed design and tests, including camera capture, recognition, record review, and manual attendance fallback.
